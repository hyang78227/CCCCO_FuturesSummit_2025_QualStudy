**1. Clear, District-Level Policy Guidance and Frameworks**
*   **Description of the unmet need:** Participants explicitly identify a lack of formalized rules and governance structures. While the need for policy is acknowledged, the transcripts do not indicate that these frameworks currently exist or that templates are being provided to solve the deficit.
*   **Who appears to have the need:** Administrators (District level) and Faculty.
*   **Session_IDs:** FS01, FS02
*   **Evidence from transcripts:** Panelist 1 states that a major challenge is "the lack of clear policy guidance at the district level". Similarly, Panelist E explicitly requests a "shared library of... sample policies".
*   **Why this unmet need matters for future programming:** Without these frameworks, institutions are stalled; Panelist 1 notes that without clear guidance and secure data access, they "can’t responsibly test AI tools".

**2. Centralized, Cross-Institutional Resource Sharing**
*   **Description of the unmet need:** There is a repeated, explicit request for a collective repository to share approved materials to prevent isolation. Stakeholders want to stop reinventing the wheel for every rubric, prompt, or curriculum module.
*   **Who appears to have the need:** Faculty and Workforce/Curriculum Developers.
*   **Session_IDs:** FS02, FS03
*   **Evidence from transcripts:** Panelist E asks for a "shared library of approved prompts". Panelist F explicitly notes that "cross-college sharing of curriculum, rubrics, and projects" is missing from statewide programming.
*   **Why this unmet need matters for future programming:** This indicates that current support is too fragmented. Establishing shared libraries would standardize "approved" use cases and facilitate the "modularized content" approaches discussed in workforce sessions.

**3. Methodologies for Evaluating Impact**
*   **Description of the unmet need:** Discussions focus heavily on the *implementation* of tools (e.g., tutoring, feedback drafts), but participants identify a lack of knowledge or discussion regarding how to measure the *effectiveness* of those tools.
*   **Who appears to have the need:** Faculty and Institutional Researchers (implied).
*   **Session_IDs:** FS02
*   **Evidence from transcripts:** When asked to identify a gap in the discussion, Panelist E states: "We talk about tools, but not enough about evaluation of impact".
*   **Why this unmet need matters for future programming:** This gap suggests that current adoption may be driven by novelty rather than proven efficacy. Future programming needs to move beyond "use cases" to evidence-based assessment of learning outcomes.

**4. Evidence-Based Examples of Employer Partnerships**
*   **Description of the unmet need:** While panelists mention "employer advisory loops" conceptually, there is a specific request for concrete examples of partnerships that are backed by data/evidence, rather than just anecdotal success.
*   **Who appears to have the need:** Workforce and Curriculum Leaders.
*   **Session_IDs:** FS03
*   **Evidence from transcripts:** Panelist G identifies "More examples of successful employer partnerships with evidence" as a key missing element.
*   **Why this unmet need matters for future programming:** Workforce preparation relies on meeting "real constraints". Without evidence-based models, institutions may implement partnership structures that fail to deliver the practical skills employers actually demand.
