Based on the session transcripts, here are the unmet needs expressed or implied by participants:

1.  **Sustainable Funding Models for AI Implementation**
    *   **Description:** There is a lack of concrete planning processes or budget models to ensure that AI tools (chatbots, fraud detection, etc.) remain financially sustainable after initial pilot funding (like COVID relief funds) runs out.
    *   **Who appears to have the need:** Administrators and Program Leads.
    *   **Session_IDs:** Cam A Session 1 v1(1), Cam D Session 2 v1.
    *   **Evidence:** In *Cam A Session 1*, an audience member asked, "What type of planning processes are you embedding to ensure that some of these practices are sustainable? ...The dollars matter." A panelist responded, "I think those tend to be higher-up decisions... I think that's probably you," and admitted to using "COVID funding to do the pilot",. In *Cam D Session 2*, an audience member noted, "Getting consistency in funding for these software products... especially when administration has to make difficult decisions... The dollars matter".
    *   **Why this matters:** Without long-term funding strategies, successful pilots run the risk of being discontinued, wasting initial investments and disrupting student services.

2.  **AI Strategies for Non-Traditional and Displaced Workers**
    *   **Description:** The current discourse and tooling heavily favor Gen Z and traditional-age students, leaving a gap in strategies for older, non-traditional learners or current workers facing immediate job displacement.
    *   **Who appears to have the need:** Non-traditional students and the Faculty/Workforce Development staff supporting them.
    *   **Session_IDs:** Cam A Session 2 v1(1).
    *   **Evidence:** An audience member explicitly asked, "What about the nontraditional students? ...we're also worried about the ones that we're not seeing yet who maybe in danger of losing their job." Panelist Christian admitted, "I'll be like directly honest like we haven't done much work with unconventional learners." Panelist Tara added, "I really, really do think more in depth work needs to be done there",,.
    *   **Why this matters:** Community colleges serve a large population of adult learners; ignoring this demographic fails to address the segment of the workforce most vulnerable to immediate AI automation.

3.  **Hardware-Agnostic Content and Stable Procurement Guidance**
    *   **Description:** Educators struggle to adopt Immersive tech (XR/VR) because content is often tied to specific hardware that quickly becomes obsolete or unsupported, creating a barrier to long-term adoption.
    *   **Who appears to have the need:** Faculty and IT Administrators.
    *   **Session_IDs:** Cam C Session 3 v1.
    *   **Evidence:** An audience member stated, "The barriers to adoption are... the content that's out there is not hardware agnostic. So, we were all buying pole lenses five or 10 years ago. And guess what? ...It's frustrating and it makes it hard for us to adopt." Panelist Inga responded, "Unfortunately you're right... we are far from that",,.
    *   **Why this matters:** Colleges cannot afford to constantly replace expensive hardware; they need software solutions that work across different devices to make investment viable.

4.  **Operational Data Governance and Standardization**
    *   **Description:** There is a critical need for standardized data formats and clear governance models across the 116 colleges to allow AI tools to function effectively, rather than "piecemealing" policies or dealing with "messy" data.
    *   **Who appears to have the need:** Administrators, IT, and Student Developers.
    *   **Session_IDs:** Cam D Session 2 v1, Cam D Session 4 v1.
    *   **Evidence:** In *Cam D Session 2*, an audience member asked, "Are there best practices? Are there models out there for institutions? Because we are just piecemealing different pieces...". In *Cam D Session 4*, a student developer stated, "If there's one thing that I want community colleges... to focus on is, standardizing the data... it's like really hard to connect these. Like the data is there, right? But it's so disconnected".
    *   **Why this matters:** Without standardized data, AI tools cannot scale across the system, and colleges risk security breaches or inefficient, hallucination-prone implementations.

5.  **Pedagogical Solutions for Student "Existential Anxiety"**
    *   **Description:** Students are experiencing deep anxiety about the value of their degrees and future careers in an AI world, and current responses focus more on tool usage rather than addressing the philosophical and emotional crisis of obsolescence.
    *   **Who appears to have the need:** Students (specifically Gen Z).
    *   **Session_IDs:** Cam C Session 5 v1.
    *   **Evidence:** An audience member asked, "A lot of Gen Z's reporting is really intense, almost existential anxiety in fear because of AI's presence. So this feeling of like, what is the value of my [work]..." The response focused on using AI as a "safe private space to heal" rather than addressing the career/value anxiety directly,.
    *   **Why this matters:** If students feel their education is pointless due to AI, enrollment and retention may suffer; they need curriculum that validates their human contribution to the workforce.

6.  **Workload Reduction Reality vs. Promise**
    *   **Description:** While AI is marketed as a tool to save time and reduce workload, faculty are experiencing the opposite—an increase in workload to manage the technology, police it, or redesign curriculum around it.
    *   **Who appears to have the need:** Faculty.
    *   **Session_IDs:** Cam D Session 2 v1, Cam C Session 3 v1.
    *   **Evidence:** A panelist in *Cam D Session 2* stated, "I'm doing far more work now that AI's here than before it was here. So, the whole idea of how it frees up your time and all that, I don't think so". In *Cam C Session 3*, a panelist noted that faculty are "overworked currently" and need to identify what they *don't* want to do, but the implementation of solutions requires significant setup.
    *   **Why this matters:** If AI adoption consistently increases rather than decreases burnout, faculty resistance will stiffen, hindering system-wide innovation.
