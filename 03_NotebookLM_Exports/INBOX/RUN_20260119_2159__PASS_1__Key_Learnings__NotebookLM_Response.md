    *   **Learning Statement:** There is a consensus that AI literacy—defined not just as technical skill but as the ability to use tools ethically, prompt with purpose, and verify outputs—must be established as a baseline competency for students, faculty, and administrators, similar to reading or numeracy.
    *   **Session_IDs:** Cam D Session 2 v1; Cam A Session 2 v1(1); Cam D Session 5 v1(1); General Session Opening Session Day 1.
    *   **Speaker Role(s):** District Administrators, Faculty, State Chancellor’s Office Executives.
    *   **Supporting Quotes:**
        *   "We need to have an understanding that we're promoting a culture of AI literacy across the system... And that means that there's a baseline of AI literacy. So, what is AI literacy? And we do have that handy acronym, PEOPLE, to remind us of the fundamentals." — *Craig Hayward*
        *   "We haven't gone outside to tell people why you should be careful with choosing AI products. So I definitely know after this conversation I'm thinking about whether we should have some webinars... to talk a little bit about... different types of AI products." — *Dr. Xuan Zhao*

2.  **The "Paradox" of AI and Climate Change**
    *   **Learning Statement:** AI presents a complex dual reality regarding the environment: it is a powerful tool for solving climate issues (e.g., optimizing traffic, reducing contrails) while simultaneously posing a massive threat due to the unprecedented water and energy consumption required by data centers.
    *   **Session_IDs:** Cam C Session 4 v1; General Session Opening Session Day 2.
    *   **Speaker Role(s):** Product Director (Google), Tech Journalist/Author.
    *   **Supporting Quotes:**
        *   "We are now at a point where the data centers are being built so quickly that we are seeing a historic uptick in energy demand around the world... So we are literally single-handedly reversing the climate progress that we made in the last decade because of this technology." — *Karen Hao*
        *   "And traffic engineers try to get to every traffic light in their city every five years to retime it... AI is really able to help accelerate their ability to get their work done to help us move in a more sustainable way around cities." — *Juliet Rothenberg*

3.  **Durable Skills Over Technical Proficiency**
    *   **Learning Statement:** Across various industries (automotive, tech, agriculture), employers are prioritizing "durable" or "soft" skills—such as curiosity, communication, and adaptability—over specific technical proficiencies, as technical tools change rapidly while human interpersonal skills remain essential.
    *   **Session_IDs:** Cam B Session 2 v1; Cam C Session 2 v1; Cam A Session 2 v1(1).
    *   **Speaker Role(s):** Industry Partners (Nuro, Rivian), Workforce Development Directors.
    *   **Supporting Quotes:**
        *   "I promise you that if you are a cantankerous genius, you will not work at Nuro, regardless of the problem you can solve... It's too hard a job to do, to do it with people whom you don't appreciate doing it with." — *Blake Williams*
        *   "While we have done a lot of work on making sure that we are bridging those skills for the... industry-relevant or role-specific skills... a huge thing that we hear... is we can't leave the durable skills behind." — *Summer*

    *   **Learning Statement:** A major immediate application of AI in higher education is building administrative capacity, specifically in detecting fraudulent applications at scale and automating repetitive "tier-one" support tasks to free up staff for high-value interactions.
    *   **Session_IDs:** Cam A Session 1 v1(1); General Session Opening Session Day 2.
    *   **Speaker Role(s):** CTOs, Deans of Student Services, Tech CEOs.
    *   **Supporting Quotes:**

5.  **AI as an Accessibility Bridge and Community Builder**
    *   **Learning Statement:** For students with disabilities, AI tools (like automated alt-text generation and summarization) and online education formats are not just conveniences but essential mechanisms for inclusion, provided that accessibility is viewed as a method of community building rather than legal compliance.
    *   **Session_IDs:** Cam A Session 4 v1(1); Cam D Session 4 v1.
    *   **Speaker Role(s):** Students, Accessibility Advocates, Industry Solutions Engineers.
    *   **Supporting Quotes:**
        *   "I think it's really important to see accessibility as something as a community builder. You're not seeing it as just following the law and avoiding a lawsuit. You're seeing it as bringing in your students and giving them a place to be in and be who they are." — *Jessica Lopez*
        *   "What ended up being originally estimated as a four month process ended up taking a week. This is the power of accessibility and using AI to make things accessible." — *Jessica Lopez*

6.  **The "Human-in-the-Loop" Necessity**
    *   **Learning Statement:** While AI can simulate empathy or automate tasks, it cannot replace human accountability or genuine connection; successful implementation requires a human to validate outputs and maintain relationships, particularly in counseling and high-risk decision-making.
    *   **Session_IDs:** Cam D Session 3 v1; Cam C Session 5 v1; Cam D Session 2 v1.
    *   **Speaker Role(s):** CEOs of EdTech, Mental Health Counselors, Faculty.
    *   **Supporting Quotes:**

        *   "And high-risk doesn't mean you can't use it, but it means there needs to be a human in the loop to break that cycle... And guess what else is in there? Educational assessment. And hiring and employment." — *Craig Hayward*
